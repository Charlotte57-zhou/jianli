# Resume Optimization Playbook

## The real job of a resume

A resume is not a complete biography.

It is an **evidence-backed prioritization document** designed to help a recruiter quickly answer:

- What kind of candidate is this?
- Why should I interview them?
- What proof supports that claim?
- What should I ask next?

## The five levers

### 1. Selection

Remove correct-but-low-value detail.

### 2. Hierarchy

Put the strongest evidence where recruiters actually see it.

### 3. Translation

Turn internal jargon into market-readable language.

Translation is one-way: internal truth controls wording, but internal audit language never appears in the recruiter-facing resume.

### 4. Evidence

Use metrics, project scope, artifacts, tests, and delivery facts.

### 5. Restraint

Do not overclaim.

Compression also cannot erase history. Grouping similar projects must preserve every independent project in the master and keep it identifiable or explicitly recorded as omitted in the internal tailoring delta.

## Advice triage

When someone gives feedback, do not ask only “Is this right?”

Ask:

- Is it right for this target role?
- Is it true for this candidate?
- Does it improve screening?
- Does it create a new misunderstanding?
- Is there evidence to support the change?
- Is the downside greater than the upside?

### Common example: “Add more metrics”

Verdict: usually adopt with modification.

Correct execution:

- add scale, complexity, quality, and delivery metrics.

Incorrect execution:

- invent efficiency gains;
- estimate annual savings and present as measured;
- attach organization-wide revenue to a small module.

### Common example: “Put school higher”

Verdict: depends on career stage and school strength.

Correct execution:

- move relevant strong education higher;
- give it readable visual weight.

Incorrect execution:

- put it at the top in tiny gray text.

### Common example: “Remove ‘not launched’”

Verdict: often adopt with modification for personal projects.

Correct execution:

- label as personal project;
- describe demo/eval/testing;
- avoid production implications;
- disclose status when asked.

Incorrect execution:

- remove the label and start writing fake customer impact.

### Common example: “Remove GitHub”

Verdict: role-dependent.

Correct execution:

- decide whether repo inspection helps or hurts the desired evaluation dimension;
- keep the repo available for interview verification.

Incorrect execution:

- hide the repository because the candidate cannot defend the project.

## First-screen heuristic

The first screen should contain enough information to form the intended candidate model.

For an early-career AI PM, a strong sequence can be:

> strong education → target identity → enterprise product depth → AI product evidence → technical collaboration.

## Resume writing is an optimization problem

Do not maximize “impressive sounding.”

Optimize claim selection in this order:

> role relevance × evidence strength × capability ownership × interview defensibility

Do not treat the formula as arithmetic. Hard boundaries remain blocking: weak ownership cannot be offset by high JD relevance, and offline evidence cannot be scored into production value.

Before the Master Resume, run the Career Positioning Audit. Before final wording, run the Claim Defensibility Matrix.

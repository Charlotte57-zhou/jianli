# Visual and Information Hierarchy

## Principle

Placement and visual weight must agree.

Moving education to the top but rendering it as tiny gray text does not make it prominent.

## Early-career candidate

Strong relevant education can appear near the top when it is a genuine screening advantage.

Example:

> **University Name** | Computer Science (211) | CET-6

Use:

- readable font size;
- bold school name;
- subtle color emphasis;
- no oversized badge unless the resume style supports it.

## First-screen priority

A recruiter should be able to infer the candidate in 5–10 seconds:

1. Who are you?
2. What role do you want?
3. What is your strongest credential?
4. What kind of product/business have you done?
5. What differentiates you?

## Avoid “web UI” over-styling

A resume is not a landing page.

Avoid:

- giant CTA-like pills;
- decorative cards that reduce density;
- badges everywhere;
- low-contrast gray critical information;
- excessive icons.

## Visual hierarchy levels

Use 3–4 levels, not 10:

- Name / primary identity
- Section heading / major platform
- Project / role
- Body / metadata

## Product-group hierarchy

When multiple subprojects belong to one platform:

- platform = stronger group heading;
- modules = smaller subheads;
- bullets = body.

This reduces repetition and visually communicates architecture.

# Fact Integrity and Evidence

This is the most important reference in the skill.

## 1. Evidence classes

### Verified

Directly supported by:

- source files;
- product documents;
- internal reports available to the candidate;
- code/tests;
- screenshots/logs;
- resume facts that have been confirmed.

Safe for strong resume wording.

### User-confirmed

The candidate explicitly confirms the fact.

Examples:

- “I participated in every stage, but did not design the system from 0→1.”
- “I led the paperless capability from 0→1.”
- “The monthly 3 million figure is real internal business data.”

Safe, but preserve the exact boundary.

### External/public

True in public sources, but not evidence that the candidate’s system handled that amount.

Example:

- the industry/platform handled a publicly reported amount of money;
- national subsidy totals;
- organization-wide transaction scale.

Can be used as context only when clearly labeled. Do not attribute it to the candidate’s module.

### Estimated

Derived from assumptions.

Example:

- monthly documents × 12 = “annual paper saved”;
- average transaction size × count = “money processed.”

Do not present as measured impact.

### Inferred

Plausible from the architecture, but not explicitly verified.

Example:

- “This must have reduced manual time.”
- “This probably improved accuracy.”

Do not present as fact.

### Unknown

Insufficient evidence.

Mark “needs verification.”

## 2. Verb ownership ladder

Use verbs as evidence-sensitive labels.

Strong ownership:

- 主导 / led
- owner
- designed from 0→1
- drove / 牵头

Use only when the candidate truly owned the outcome.

Medium ownership:

- 负责 / responsible for
- independently delivered
- designed

Use when the candidate owned a defined scope.

Participation:

- 深度参与 / deeply participated
- participated in
- collaborated on
- supported

Use when the candidate contributed significantly but did not own the whole system.

Never upgrade verbs just to sound senior.

## 3. 0→1 is not a synonym for “I worked on it”

A project is 0→1 for the candidate only when they actually participated in defining and delivering a capability from nonexistence to usable state.

A mature system inherited from another colleague is **not** 0→1.

A new module inside a mature platform **can** be 0→1.

## 4. Public scale vs system scale

Bad:

> Supported 120 billion RMB annual settlement.

when the 120 billion comes from a public platform report rather than the candidate’s module.

Better:

> Covered 26 provincial operating regions and processed ~3 million business-invoice relationships per month.

when those numbers are verified for the actual system.

## 5. Estimates vs outcomes

Bad:

> Saved 60 million sheets of paper annually.

when this is estimated from document count.

Better:

> Paperless generation and electronic archiving covered at least 6 million high-frequency business documents per month.

This is a scale metric, not a fabricated environmental outcome.

## 6. System boundary integrity

Do not merge adjacent systems.

If System A calculates settlement and System B handles funding application/allocation, do not write that System A owns funding application/allocation merely because the workflows intersect.

## 7. Employer vs client platform

Bad:

> Worked on Employer X’s Shared Finance Platform

when the platform is actually the client’s construction program.

Better:

> Worked at Employer X; participated in Client Y’s Shared Finance Platform projects.

## 8. Personal project status

A personal project can be highly valuable without production deployment.

Use evidence such as:

- runnable demo;
- offline eval;
- test suite;
- product design;
- architecture;
- open-source release.

Do not convert these into:

- customer adoption;
- production accuracy;
- real financial savings;
- live system performance.
